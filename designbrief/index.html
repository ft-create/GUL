01 / The mark

Five domes radiating from the sun. The petal is not like a dome — it is the dome.

Gul is Persian and Urdu for flower — and, said on its own, the rose. The name is already the mark: five petals, one sun at the centre, five prayers in a day. Nothing had to be invented to get from the word to the drawing.

The dome silhouette, stretched: flat base, haunches swelling past the base, a point at the top. Each dome belongs to one named prayer and is fixed to its position — Fajr at the top, then clockwise — so the shape of a day is legible at a glance. A prayer you have noted is a dome filled cream; one you have not yet is the same dome, outlined. Five-fold symmetry holds at every state, so one look tells you where you are in the day.

02 / Design + identity

The sun is the palette. Everything else is a small fixed set.

Gul is the flower — the rose. Five prayers, five petals: a day fully noted is a flower in full bloom, and that is the whole metaphor. Deliberately not a crescent: the crescent-and-star is the most-used mark in this category, so reaching for it buys recognition and spends distinctiveness in the same move.

−40° to +70° · the twilight stops sit near −22°, where Fajr and Isha fall

Eleven stops interpolated by real solar altitude — computed, not chosen — and the setting sun runs warmer than the rising sun at the same angle, because dusk should not look like dawn. From Isha to first light the disc turns grey (#9AA6AE): the same circle, only its colour changes, so the mark never changes shape. Nothing else in the product may be warm.

Gold is the sun and nothing else. No gold buttons, no gold borders. Green means a prayer was noted and nothing else. A lapse is earth. There is no red anywhere in the product.

02

Type does one job each. Instrument Serif for prayer names, times and anything meant to be read slowly. DM Sans for every interface element, labels uppercase at .22em. Two families, no third — a type system this small only stays legible if nothing is added to it.

03

The voice is plain, quiet, second person. It tells you the time, the place, and what you have noted. Nothing is dressed up: a window is open or it is not, and the app says which.

04

If it can be tapped, it is the whole row. Noting a prayer is the one thing this app exists to do, so its target is never smaller than a full-width row at 56px. Five chips on one line, each only as wide as the word inside it, is a thumb aiming at seven characters — that was the first version of Today and it was wrong. Silence is the default state: an empty ring says not yet without five rows announcing what you have not done.

03 / The world clock

One sun. Whichever city you are standing in.

The five times are not fetched from anywhere — they are computed on the device from your coordinates and the date, so the app works on a plane and in a basement. Change the place and the whole day changes shape: how steeply the sun climbs, how long it stays up, how late Isha finally lands. Press a city below and watch the same day rebuild itself.

Ten conventions are supported — MWL, ISNA, Egypt, Umm al-Qura, Karachi, Tehran, Jafari, Diyanet, Dubai, Singapore — with Standard and Ḥanafī Asr. Above roughly 48° the sun may never reach the Fajr or Isha angle in midsummer; when that happens the app fills the gap by a stated rule and says on screen that it has estimated, rather than quietly inventing a time.

04 / The brief

An easy way to keep track of your five.

Purpose built

Prayer times that are locally accurate wherever you are, reminders on your own terms, and a quiet private place to note your five prayers a day.

The product answer

A day is one flower. Note a prayer and its dome fills; the sun at the centre reports what the sky is actually doing. One glance tells you where you are in the day.

Today, and the month

Under the flower, one row per prayer — name, real time, and whether you noted it — where the whole row is the button. Then a timetable with every window and when it closes, and a month view where each day is its own small flower, so the shape of a month is legible, not just today.

Privacy posture

Records live on the device and work with no account and no network. Accounts are opt-in: sign in and your notes sync, encrypted in transit, readable only by you. Coordinates are used locally and never uploaded.

Accuracy posture

Times are computed astronomically on the device — NOAA/Meeus solar position, ten common conventions, Standard and Ḥanafī Asr, and honest high-latitude rules that always say when they have estimated. No API key, works offline, anywhere on Earth.

The line it holds

The app knows where the sun is; it cannot know whether a prayer happened, so what you note is yours to enter. Questions of practice go to a qualified scholar, not to software.

05 / Product path

From a working web app to a build that can wake you at Fajr.

Now — the web app

Prayer times anywhere on Earth, accounts and cross-device sync, marking, history and the live mark all work in the browser. It installs to a home screen and runs offline.

Next — the native build

Scheduled reminders are the reason the native build exists: a web page cannot reliably wake anyone before dawn. Same mark, same engine, same account.

Market-ready release

Account recovery, consent and privacy documentation, a licensed Adhan recitation with its attribution, and store distribution.

Status. The web app is live and in use, with accounts and sync. The native build and the store release are a deliberate next phase, not a claim already made.
