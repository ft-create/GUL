Pray with the sun

A prayer app whose palette is the sky. Five prayers, five petals, and a sun at the centre whose colour is computed from its real altitude rather than chosen — so the interface changes with the day, and the mark is a working instrument rather than a logo.

01 / The mark

The logo is the mechanic, not a metaphor for it.

A dome, five petals inside it, and the sun at the centre where the petals meet. Each petal belongs to one named prayer and is fixed to its position — Fajr at the top, then clockwise — so the shape of a day is legible at a glance. A prayer you have noted is a petal filled cream; one you have not is the same petal, outlined. Never smaller, never wilted, never red.

Five noted · one noted · Isha through to first light

02 / Design + identity

The sun is the palette. Everything else is a small fixed set.

Gul (گُل) is the rose. The Qur'an's reward for those who guard their prayers is a garden — Gul is what grows in it. Deliberately not a crescent: the crescent-and-star is the most-used mark in this category and is Ottoman heraldry rather than anything scriptural, so reaching for it buys recognition and spends distinctiveness in the same move.

−40° to +70° · the twilight stops sit near −22°, where Fajr and Isha fall

Eleven stops interpolated by solar altitude, and the setting sun runs warmer than the rising sun at the same angle — dusk should not look like dawn. Nothing else in the product may be warm.

Gold is the sun and nothing else. No gold buttons, no gold borders. Green means a prayer was noted and nothing else. A lapse is earth. There is no red anywhere in the product.

02

Two motion laws, never mixed. The sun is linear at 600ms, because it does not accelerate for anybody and moves whether or not the app is open. A petal eases at 760ms, because it answers a person — and fires only when you note a prayer, never on a timer.

03

Type does one job each. Instrument Serif for prayer names, times and anything meant to be read slowly. DM Sans for every interface element. Amiri for Qur'anic Arabic, at double line height, never animated and never decorative.

03 / Design brief

Make prayer easy to keep without making it something to score.

Purpose built

Prayer times that are locally accurate, reminders on the user's own terms, and a quiet private place to note five prayers a day — with no claim about anyone's standing.

The product answer

A day is one flower. Note a prayer and its petal opens; the sun at the centre reports what the sky is actually doing. The record is the reward.

What it refuses to do

No streak, no percentage, no perfect day, no aggregate anywhere. Nothing is auto-marked. A window that passed unnoted is simply unopened, and that is all.

Privacy posture

Records live on the device and work with no account and no network. Sync is opt-in and encrypted before it leaves; coordinates are used locally and never uploaded.

Accessibility

Colour is never the only state indicator — every state carries a glyph and a label. 44×44 minimum targets throughout, and reduced motion collapses the petal and dome to 1ms while the sun keeps moving, because it is information.

The line it holds

The app cannot know whether a prayer happened, so it never says one did. No claim of divine reward, spiritual standing or prayer validity — questions of practice go to a qualified scholar, not to software.

04 / Product path

From a working web preview to a build that can wake you at Fajr.

Now — web preview

Prayer times, marking, history and the live mark all work in the browser. It installs to a home screen and runs offline.

Next — the native build

Scheduled reminders are the reason the native build exists: a web page cannot reliably wake anyone before dawn.

Market-ready release

Account recovery, consent and privacy documentation, a licensed Adhan recitation with its attribution, and store distribution.

Status. Web preview is live and in use. The native build and the store release are a deliberate next phase, not a claim already made.
